Foehn valley similarity index

Logic and data flow of the soarWRF foehn valley index with its pressure gate, as implemented on the branch foehn-valley-index.

The index estimates, for each grid point and each hour, how much the model surface wind looks like foehn flowing down a known foehn valley, when the pressure difference across the Alps drives foehn in that direction.

1. Overview

The index combines three ingredients:

  1. Geometry. There are 83 digitised foehn flow lines in Switzerland and its surroundings: 65 south foehn lines and 18 north foehn lines. Each line runs in the direction the foehn flows.
  2. Wind. Within 10 km of a flow line, the WRF 10 m wind is projected onto the direction of the nearest line. The result is the wind component blowing down the valley, in m/s.
  3. Pressure gate. The mean-sea-level pressure (MSLP) difference between a point south and a point north of the Alps decides which foehn direction is plausible. It also scales the result: a line that contradicts the pressure difference gets an index of 0.

Two values are reported at every grid point and for every valley:

  • similarity, called s in the JSON: how strongly the wind blows along the flow line, between 0 and 1, without the pressure gate.
  • index, called i: the similarity multiplied by the pressure gate, between 0 and 1. This is the value shown on the map.

Keeping both makes it possible to explain a low index. For example, the wind may fit the valley well while the pressure difference points the other way.

In short:

index = ramp(w · t, 2, 6 m/s) × [d ≤ 10 km] × ramp(|Δp|, 2, 6 hPa) × [line direction matches the sign of Δp] ramp(x, a, b) = clamp((x − a) / (b − a), 0, 1)

Here w · t is the wind component along the line (wind speed × cos θ), d the distance to the line, and Δp the cross-Alpine pressure difference. The parameters are heuristic and not calibrated against observations (see section 11).

The index is WRF only. It is computed for both WRF zones: alps-overview (6 km, file d02) and central-alps (2 km, file d03).

2. End-to-end data flow

Steps in wrf/DataPipeline.processFile, run once per WRF input file (zone):

  1. NetCdf.read builds the forecast grid for all hours. This step is unchanged by the feature.
  2. withFoehnValleys calls FoehnValleys.diagnose with the grid and the latitude/longitude of each cell. It returns a copy of the grid where every cell near a flow line has valleyFoehn filled in, plus the per-valley scores. It is a separate method, so the original grid can be garbage-collected.
  3. generateRasterImagesAndVectorTiles writes the usual rasters plus foehn-valley-similarity, because the WRF pipeline passes Raster.wrfRasters.
  4. generateLocationForecasts writes the per-location JSON. The fv block is added where valleyFoehn is defined.
  5. foehn-valleys.json is written next to the zone's other outputs.

3. Input: the foehn flow lines

Source file: frontend/src/data/foehn-valleys.geojson. It is a FeatureCollection of LineString features in WGS84 (longitude, latitude). The lines were digitised from a schematic map and adjusted to the topography. Positional accuracy is about 1–3 km.

PropertyUsed byMeaning
idbackend, frontendStable identifier, for example S18 or N00. It links each line to its score in foehn-valleys.json.
foehnbackend, frontend"south" or "north": the foehn type the line represents.
stylebackend, frontend"solid" (60 lines) or "dotted" (23 lines, where foehn is observed less often). Dotted lines are drawn dashed; the index treats both styles the same.
length_km, from_near, to_nearnoneDescriptive only.

Orientation matters. Each line runs from its upstream end (tail) to its downstream end (arrow tip). A south foehn line therefore points roughly north, and a north foehn line roughly south.

3.1 Densification (densify)

When FoehnValleys is first used, each line is turned into evenly spaced samples. In total there are about 1,200 samples.

  • Each segment is cut into ceil(length / 1.5 km) pieces. A sample is placed at the start of each piece.
  • Each sample stores the unit tangent (east, north) of its segment. The east component is scaled by cos(mean latitude), so the direction is correct in kilometres rather than degrees.
  • The final point (the arrow tip) is added as an extra sample with the direction of the last segment, so the downstream end of the valley is scored too.
  • Each sample also stores its valley id and its foehn direction.

3.2 Spatial index (GeoBucketIndex)

To keep the nearest-neighbour searches cheap, samples and grid cells are put into latitude/longitude buckets about 10 km wide (the corridor width). A query looks at its own bucket and the 8 around it. That is guaranteed to include every item within the 10 km search radius. The bucket width in longitude is computed at the highest latitude present, so a bucket is never narrower than 10 km.

Distances use a local flat-earth approximation: 111.2 km per degree of latitude, and longitude degrees scaled by cos(reference latitude). The reference latitude is the mean latitude of the grid.

4. Cross-Alpine pressure difference

The gate uses the MSLP difference between a southern and a northern reference point. There are two regions, chosen by the longitude of the grid point:

RegionApplies toSouth pointNorth point
Lugano–Zurichlongitude < 10.2° E46.00° N, 8.96° E (Lugano)47.38° N, 8.57° E (Zurich)
Bolzano–Innsbrucklongitude ≥ 10.2° E46.50° N, 11.33° E (Bolzano)47.27° N, 11.39° E (Innsbruck)

The calculation, in pressureDifferences:

  1. Pick the cells around each reference point, once per run. pointsAround collects the grid cells within 20 km of the point. If there are fewer than 3, the radius grows by 10 km at a time, up to 150 km. Only cells with MSLP > 0 in the first hour count, because the WRF post-processing writes 0 for MSLP above about 800 m. If no radius gives 3 cells, that point is unavailable, and so is its region.
  2. Compute the difference for each hour. For each available region: Δp = mean MSLP(south cells) − mean MSLP(north cells), in hPa.
  3. Assign a value to each grid cell. A cell takes the Δp of the region that matches its longitude. If that region is unavailable, it takes the first available region. If no region is available, Δp is NaN.

A positive Δp means pressure is higher on the south side, which drives south foehn. A negative Δp drives north foehn.

4.1 The gate

gate(Δp) = ramp(|Δp|, 2 hPa, 6 hPa) (0 if Δp is NaN) pressureGatedIndex(similarity, direction, Δp) = similarity × gate(Δp) if (direction = south and Δp > 0) or (direction = north and Δp < 0) 0 otherwise (wrong sign, Δp = 0, or NaN)

Below 2 hPa the gate is 0, and from 6 hPa upwards it is 1. In between it rises linearly; for example, at 4 hPa it is 0.5.

5. Grid-point index

For each grid cell, which is done once per run:

  • nearestTemplates finds the nearest south line sample and the nearest north line sample within 10 km (the corridor). Either one may be missing.

Then for each hour and each cell:

  1. Choose a line (chooseTemplate):
    • If Δp > 0, prefer the south sample and fall back to the north one.
    • If Δp < 0, prefer the north sample and fall back to the south one.
    • If Δp is 0 or NaN, take the closer of the two.
    • If there is no sample within 10 km, the cell is outside every corridor and gets no valleyFoehn at all: the raster is transparent there and the popup says "No foehn valley nearby".
  2. Similarity (windSimilarity). Let (u, v) be the 10 m wind and (e, n) the unit tangent of the line:
    w · t = u·e + v·n (wind component along the line, m/s; = speed × cos θ) similarity = ramp(w · t, 2 m/s, 6 m/s)
    An along-line wind below 2 m/s, a crosswind, or a wind blowing up the valley gives 0. From 6 m/s along the line the similarity is 1. Direction and speed are combined in one quantity: a strong wind tolerates a larger angle than a weak one. At 8 m/s, for example, the similarity is 1 up to about 41° and 0 from about 76°.
  3. Corridor. Every cell within 10 km of a line gets the full similarity; there is no further distance weighting.
  4. Index: index = pressureGatedIndex(similarity, direction of the chosen line, Δp of the cell).

The factors of the index each range from 0 to 1. The second panel shows how the along-line ramp translates into an angular tolerance for an 8 m/s wind. Hover over a curve to read its value.

The along-line ramp and the corridor give the similarity. The pressure gate (section 4.1) then multiplies the similarity into the index.

Each cell stores the result as Forecast.valleyFoehn = Diagnostics(similarity, index, direction, valleyId, distanceMeters).

Why fall back to the line of the other direction? When south foehn is expected but only a north foehn line is nearby, the cell still reports that line and its wind similarity, while the index stays 0. The popup can then show why the index is zero: there is a valley nearby, but the pressure pattern doesn't support foehn there.

6. Per-valley hourly score

The score of each flow line feeds the line overlay on the map.

  1. Matching cells, once per run. Each sample of the line is matched to its nearest grid cell within 10 km (nearestGridCell). Lines with no matching cell are outside the domain and are left out of foehn-valleys.json.
  2. Score for each hour (scoreValley):
    similarity = mean over matched samples of ramp(w · t, 2, 6 m/s) at its cell meanΔp = mean of the finite Δp values at those cells (NaN if none) index = pressureGatedIndex(similarity, direction of the line, meanΔp)

Each sample is compared with the wind in its nearest cell. A line crossing the 10.2° E boundary uses the mean Δp of both regions, weighted by how many of its samples lie on each side.

7. Backend outputs

All files are written under <output>/7/wrf/<init>/<zone>/, where 7 is the current forecast format version.

7.1 Raster foehn-valley-similarity/<hour>.png

This is an RGBA PNG of the grid-point index, defined in Raster.wrfRasters as gfsRasters plus this raster, so GFS doesn't produce it. Cells without valleyFoehn are encoded as 0. The colour breakpoints are:

Values are assigned to classes by the standard GeoTrellis ColorMap rules. The legend in frontend/src/layers/FoehnValleys.tsx must match these colours.

7.2 Per-location JSON: the fv block

The block is added to each hour of the location forecast files (locations/*.json), only where the cell has valleyFoehn:

"fv": {
  "s":  612,     // similarity, per mille (0–1000), null if not finite
  "i":  598,     // index, per mille, null if not finite
  "k":  0,       // direction of the chosen line: 0 = south, 1 = north
  "id": "S18",   // id of the chosen line
  "d":  3400     // distance to it, in metres
}

7.3 foehn-valleys.json

There is one file per zone and run, covering all hours. hours[n] is the hour offset n, the same index as the raster file names.

{
  "valleys": [
    {
      "id": "S18",
      "foehn": "south",
      "hours": [
        { "s": 712, "i": 712, "dp": 58 },   // s, i per mille; dp in tenths of hPa (5.8 hPa)
        ...
      ]
    },
    ...
  ]
}

Only lines with at least one sample within 10 km of the zone's grid are listed.

8. Frontend

8.1 Layer

foehnValleySimilarityLayer in src/layers/FoehnValleys.tsx is defined as follows:

  • Key and raster path foehn-valley-similarity, registered in Layers.tsx.
  • The map key is a colour scale from 20 % to 100 % that uses the backend colours.
  • When clicking the map, the popup shows:
    • Valley match: the index.
    • Along-valley wind: the similarity before the gate.
    • Foehn direction and Nearest flow line: the line id and its distance.
    These values are parsed from fv into DetailedForecast.valleyFoehn.
  • The layer button appears in the overlay menu only when soarWRF is selected (LayersSelector.tsx).
  • The raster is shown like other primary layers: the current hour's PNG, at the default overlay opacity of 0.35.

8.2 Flow-line overlay

The lines are shown only while the valley layer is the active primary layer. The steps are:

  1. App.tsx creates a resource whose URL is urlOfFoehnValleys(zone) while the layer is selected and the overlay is enabled, and undefined otherwise. fetchFoehnValleys returns undefined when the request fails, for example for runs produced before this feature.
  2. An effect converts the data to a Map<id, index> for the current hour (foehnValleyScores) and calls mapHooks.setFoehnValleyScores. Changing the hour only re-applies the scores; it doesn't fetch the file again.
  3. Map.ts loads the bundled GeoJSON on first use only. It sets each feature's foehnIndex property and refreshes the layer. Passing undefined hides the layer. The line layer sits above the raster and below the wind barbs.

10. Tests and verification

backend/common/src/test/scala/org/soaringmeteo/FoehnValleysTestSuite.scala has 10 tests.

Unit tests of the building blocks:

  • The GeoJSON loads with 83 lines (65 south, 18 north), and every sample has a valley id.
  • Densified south lines point north, and north lines point south.
  • The similarity is a ramp of the along-line wind: 1 at 10 m/s along the line, 0.5 at 4 m/s along the line and at 8 m/s with a 60° angle, and 0 at 2 m/s, for an opposite wind, or for calm.
  • The line choice follows the sign of Δp, with a fallback to the other direction.
  • The gate zeroes a line whose direction contradicts Δp, and also zeroes it when Δp is NaN.
  • The JSON encoding uses per mille and tenths of hPa.

End-to-end tests of diagnose. They use a synthetic 15 × 33 grid over Lugano–Zurich (8.4–9.1° E, 45.9–47.5° N) with a uniform northward 10 m/s wind:

  • MSLP 1010 hPa south and 1004 hPa north: Δp is exactly +6 hPa, south valleys keep index = similarity, and north valleys get 0.
  • Reversed pressure: south valleys get index 0, while their similarity stays above 0.
  • Δp of +4 hPa: the index is reduced but not zero.
  • MSLP of 0 everywhere (no data): Δp is NaN and every index is 0.

Measured statement coverage of FoehnValleys.scala is 96 %. The uncovered parts are error branches: a missing or unparseable resource, an unknown foehn value, and non-finite values in the JSON encoding.

When the branch was built, the WRF run 2026-11-13 12Z (+42 h) was regenerated with the branch code. Its foehn-valleys.json, the foehn-valley-similarity rasters, and a sample of the location fv blocks were identical to those of the development version they were extracted from.

11. Limitations and known behaviour

  • Two pressure regions only. Valleys far from Lugano–Zurich or Bolzano–Innsbruck (for example in the western or eastern Alps) still use one of these two pairs.
  • MSLP coverage. MSLP is 0 above about 800 m in the WRF post-processing. If no valid cells exist near a reference point, its region falls back to the other region, or Δp becomes NaN and every index is 0.
  • Surface wind only. The index uses the 10 m wind. It doesn't check stability, dryness, crest-level wind or descent.
  • Line accuracy. The digitised lines are accurate to about 1–3 km. The 10 km corridor absorbs that, but small side valleys can be missed or confused.
  • Sharp corridor edge. The index drops from its full value to 0 at 10 km from a line, so the map shows clear-cut valley strips.
  • Strong crosswinds. A strong wind at a large angle still has a component along the line: 15 m/s at 80° gives 2.6 m/s, so a similarity of 0.15.
  • Heuristic parameters. The corridor width and the two ramps are educated guesses, not calibrated. A natural next step is to tune them against the MeteoSwiss foehn index at valley stations (Dürr 2008) over past runs.
  • WRF only. If the layer was selected with soarWRF and the user then switches to GFS, the layer stays selected but there is no raster to show.
  • Older runs. Runs produced before this feature have no fv block and no foehn-valleys.json. The popup then shows "No foehn valley nearby", and the lines are hidden.
  • Translations. The new texts exist only in messages/en.json.
